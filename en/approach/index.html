Artistic Approach

Tradition, Technique, and Spatial Imagination

For George Klapsa Gogolashvili, monumental painting is inseparable from the architecture that surrounds it.

Rather than approaching the wall as a neutral surface, his practice considers the scale, proportions, light, and symbolic purpose of an interior.

His artistic method is shaped by a continuing engagement with Georgian medieval painting and the techniques of historical muralists.

Historical Sources

The monumental painting traditions of Georgia, particularly those associated with Ubisi and Gelati, have served as important references in Gogolashvili's ecclesiastical work.

These sources inform his research into composition, iconographic structure, and the visual language of sacred art.

Direct Work on the Wall

Gogolashvili has described a working process that places particular emphasis on preparatory studies and the direct transfer of designs onto architectural surfaces.

Hand-drawn lines, proportional relationships, and careful compositional planning are central to this approach.

Color and Iconography

Within sacred painting, color and expression carry significance beyond their decorative function. Gogolashvili approaches these elements as part of a wider visual and symbolic language.

Painting and Architecture

His experience with theatrical and architectural decoration informs an understanding of monumental art as an integral element of its environment.

The resulting approach places artistic composition in dialogue with structure, scale, and architectural purpose.
