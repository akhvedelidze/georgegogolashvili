The Artist

From Tbilisi to New York

Born and raised in the Vake district of Tbilisi, Georgia, George Klapsa Gogolashvili developed his artistic identity within a cultural environment shaped by painting, theatre, and the traditions of monumental art.

His early professional experiences included work in theatrical settings, where he encountered the demands of scenography, large-scale decorative painting, and the visual organization of space. In a published interview, Gogolashvili identified painters Zaur Golava and Muraz Murvanidze as important influences on his artistic development.

He worked in environments associated with Tbilisi's Musical Comedy Theatre, the Opera Theatre, and Rezo Gabriadze's Marionette Theatre before completing his studies at the Tbilisi State Academy of Arts, specializing in monumental wall painting.

In 1993, Gogolashvili moved to the United States, where his professional practice expanded into architectural and decorative projects in New York. His work involved interior compositions, ornamental elements, and the integration of visual art into architectural environments.

These experiences reinforced a central concern of his artistic practice: the relationship between painted imagery and the space it inhabits.

Georgian Sacred Art in Manhattan

A defining chapter in Gogolashvili's documented career is his work at the Cathedral of the Holy Virgin Protection on East Second Street in Manhattan.

His involvement with the cathedral began with the restoration of a historical icon and developed into an extensive engagement with sacred wall painting.

For the cathedral's Mystical Supper composition, Gogolashvili explored medieval Georgian painting, particularly the fresco tradition associated with the fourteenth-century master Damiane at the Ubisi Monastery.

He subsequently painted archangels and angelic hosts for the sanctuary. In a 2022 interview he described that work as completed in 2018. A certificate of recognition dated October 13, 2019 records the creation and installation of those murals.

These works provide an example of how historical Georgian visual traditions can be interpreted within a different architectural and cultural context.

An Artistic Practice Grounded in Research

Gogolashvili's approach combines academic training, direct engagement with historical sources, and traditional methods of working on architectural surfaces.

In describing his process, he emphasizes the importance of preparatory drawing, transferring compositions to walls by hand, and considering the symbolic role of color and expression in ecclesiastical imagery.

His work reflects an ongoing conversation between artistic inheritance and contemporary practice, bringing together the disciplines of painting, decorative composition, and architectural design.
